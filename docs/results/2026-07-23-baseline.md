# libumem cross-arch baseline matrix — 2026-07-23

Authoritative allocator scaling baseline (Task C2), produced with the
stabilized harness from Task C1 (warm-up discard + median-of-5 + CoV, pinned
via `numactl`). Data lives under `docs/results/<date>-<instance>-<arch>/`.

| role | instance | arch | vCPU | governor | umem status |
|------|----------|------|------|----------|-------------|
| intel-lo | c7i.2xlarge | x86_64 | 8 | (virt, none) | ok (shared box, noisy) |
| intel-hi | c7i.metal-48xl | x86_64 | 192 | performance | ok — **authoritative x86** |
| arm-lo | c7g.2xlarge | aarch64 | 8 | (virt, none) | **SIGSEGV** |
| arm-hi | c8g.metal-48xl | aarch64 | 192 | (metal, none) | **SIGSEGV** |

Each matrix.toml: `{single,multi,prodcons,frag}` × threads
`{1,2,4,8,16,32,64,128,192}` (capped at vCPU) × sizes
`{16:64,64:256,256:1024,1024:4096}` × `{libc,umem}`. 10M ops/point,
5 measured runs + 1 warm-up, pinned. `ops_cov`/`unstable` recorded per point.

---

## Headline findings

1. **umem does not run on aarch64 in this build.** Every umem allocation
   SIGSEGVs in the rseq fast path — `umem_rseq_alloc_fastpath` at
   `umem_rseq_aarch64.S:168` (confirmed under gdb on c7g.2xlarge). Both arm
   roles therefore have **libc-only** matrices. This is a correctness defect
   for Workstream B/D, surfaced by the baseline sweep. The baseline reports
   the aarch64 **libc** curve as the reference the fixed umem must beat.

2. **On x86, umem's `multi` workload stops scaling at 2 threads** and its tail
   latency degrades catastrophically at high core counts. `prodcons` is the
   opposite story — umem *beats* libc up to ~8–16 threads with far lower p99.

3. **Single-thread x86**: umem is faster in throughput (7.35 vs 6.06 Mops/s,
   +21%) but ~1.8× per-op latency (p50 29 ns vs 16 ns), matching the review.

## x86 (intel-hi, c7i.metal-48xl, 192 vCPU, performance governor — AUTHORITATIVE)

### `multi` (all threads alloc+free the same size-class), size 64:256

| threads | libc Mops/s | umem Mops/s | umem/libc | umem p99 (ns) | umem p999 (ns) | libc p999 (ns) |
|--------:|------------:|------------:|----------:|--------------:|---------------:|---------------:|
| 1   | 5.90 | 6.71 | 114% | 47     | 50        | 18   |
| 2   | 6.99 | 4.20 | 60%  | 107    | 284       | 28   |
| 4   | 2.98 | 2.44 | 82%  | 1088   | 3648      | 257  |
| 8   | 2.64 | 2.18 | 82%  | 1479   | 4230      | 252  |
| 16  | 2.46 | 2.22 | 90%  | 1091   | 3621      | 266  |
| 32  | 2.41 | 2.17 | 90%  | 1239   | 6498      | 124  |
| 64  | 1.90 | 1.48 | 78%  | 2129   | 11241     | 406  |
| 128 | 2.29 | 1.22 | 53%  | 11324  | 55243     | 982  |
| 192 | 1.48 | 1.12 | 75%  | 247340 | **1832166** | 1949 |

- **umem `multi` peaks at 1 thread and never recovers** — throughput drops the
  moment a second thread contends (6.71 → 4.20 Mops/s), and falls monotonically.
- **Tail latency is the real failure**: umem p999 climbs 50 ns → 3.6 µs (4 thr)
  → 55 µs (128 thr) → **1.83 ms at 192 threads**, ~940× libc's 1949 ns. This is
  the "p999 ~10× glibc" regression from the review, far worse at high core count.
- (256:1024 tells the same story: umem cliff at 2 threads, p999 → 124 µs at 192.)

### `prodcons` (producers alloc, consumers free — cross-thread handoff), size 64:256

| threads | libc Mops/s | umem Mops/s | umem/libc | umem p99 (ns) | libc p99 (ns) |
|--------:|------------:|------------:|----------:|--------------:|--------------:|
| 2   | 7.67 | 5.02 | 65%  | 3148  | 167   |
| 4   | 4.04 | 9.90 | 245% | 310   | 5353  |
| 8   | 6.69 | 9.40 | 140% | 312   | 5370  |
| 16  | 8.51 | 7.89 | 93%  | 340   | 3742  |
| 32  | 4.98 | 4.55 | 91%  | 466   | 2766  |
| 64  | 4.24 | 3.74 | 88%  | 855   | 4722  |
| 128 | 2.19 | 2.53 | 116% | 7450  | 14748 |
| 192 | 1.73 | 1.63 | 94%  | 24582 | 45234 |

- **umem wins `prodcons` up to ~8–16 threads** (2.4× libc at 4 thr) with an
  order-of-magnitude lower p99 (310 ns vs 5353 ns) — the magazine/depot design
  is doing its job here. umem `prodcons` peaks at 4–8 threads and stops scaling
  around 8–16, then converges to libc.

### Scaling cliff (x86, authoritative)

| workload | umem peak | umem stops scaling | libc stops scaling |
|----------|----------:|-------------------:|-------------------:|
| multi 64:256    | 1 thr | **2 thr** | 4 thr |
| multi 256:1024  | 1 thr | **2 thr** | 2 thr |
| prodcons 64:256 | 4 thr | **8 thr** | 4 thr |
| prodcons 256:1024 | 8 thr | **16 thr** | 4 thr |

**On x86, umem `multi` stops scaling at 2 threads; `prodcons` at 8–16 threads.**
This is worse than the review's "4→8 on 8-core" — with a real 192-vCPU box and
pinned/warmed measurement, the `multi` regression begins at the *second* thread.

## aarch64 (arm-hi, c8g.metal-48xl Graviton4, 192 vCPU — libc reference only)

umem SIGSEGVs, so this is the **libc** curve umem must eventually beat.

### libc `multi`, size 64:256

| threads | libc Mops/s | p99 (ns) | p999 (ns) |
|--------:|------------:|---------:|----------:|
| 1   | 6.14 | 37  | 37  |
| 2   | 5.08 | 37  | 37  |
| 4   | 4.27 | 38  | 39  |
| 8   | 3.77 | 37  | 40  |
| 16  | 3.78 | 38  | 42  |
| 32  | 3.44 | 38  | 101 |
| 64  | 3.57 | 53  | 178 |
| 128 | 3.38 | 175 | 567 |
| 192 | 3.15 | 244 | 685 |

- Graviton4 libc `multi` degrades **gently** (6.14 → 3.15 Mops/s over 1→192
  threads) and keeps p999 under 700 ns even at 192 threads — much flatter than
  x86 libc, and dramatically better than x86 umem's 1.8 ms tail. (No SMT + a
  large uniform LLC helps.) libc `prodcons` scales to ~19.8 Mops/s at 16 threads.

- **arm-lo (c7g.2xlarge, 8 vCPU)** shows the same shape at small scale
  (libc-only; umem SIGSEGV).

## What umem must fix (feeds Workstream B/D)

1. **aarch64 rseq fast path crashes** (`umem_rseq_aarch64.S:168`) — umem is
   unusable on Graviton. Highest-priority correctness bug.
2. **x86 `multi` scaling cliff at 2 threads** + **catastrophic p999 tail** at
   high core counts (1.83 ms @ 192 threads). The contention path (rseq
   restarts / depot lock / magazine thrash when many threads hammer one
   size-class) is the target for D1/D2.
3. `prodcons` is the proof the design *can* win under contention — the fix
   should bring `multi` toward `prodcons`-like behavior without regressing it.

## Reproduce

```
# per role (intel-hi shown); arm roles will show umem SIGSEGV skipped per point
./scripts/ec2/launch.sh intel-hi && ./scripts/ec2/bootstrap.sh intel-hi
./scripts/ec2/run-remote.sh intel-hi \
  'rm -f libtool config.status aclocal.m4 && ./autogen.sh && ./configure && \
   make -j$(nproc) test/bench/bench_main && cd test/bench && bash matrix.sh libc umem'
./scripts/ec2/terminate.sh intel-hi
```

Data: `docs/results/2026-07-23-{c7i.metal-48xl-x86_64,c8g.metal-48xl-aarch64,c7i.2xlarge-x86_64,c7g.2xlarge-aarch64}/matrix.toml`.

Note: intel-lo (c7i.2xlarge) is a **shared** box run concurrently with other
agents; its numbers carry more run-to-run noise (some points flagged
`unstable`) and are not authoritative for scaling — use intel-hi for x86.
