SIMD Threshold Benchmark Results
=================================
Date: 2026-04-09
Platform: x86_64 with AVX2
CPU: [Run on your hardware to fill in]

Key Findings:

1. Magazine Scanning (umem_mag_scan_notnull):
   - Sizes 1-2: Scalar wins (6-17% faster)
   - Sizes 4+: SIMD wins (50-163% faster)
   - Recommended threshold: 4 elements
   - Conservative threshold: 8 elements (to be safe)

2. Magazine Initialization (umem_mag_init_fast):
   - UNEXPECTED: Scalar/memset is FASTER for most sizes\!
   - Only sizes 4, 8, 12, 16, 20 show SIMD benefit
   - Sizes 15, 31, 47, 63, 95, 127: Scalar 40-50% faster
   - Problem: Likely unaligned stores or memory ordering issues
   - Recommended threshold: 64+ elements (or disable SIMD for init)

3. Early Exit Performance:
   - SIMD wins even with early exits at positions 0, 2, 4
   - AVX2 parallel comparison is effective
   - No need for special handling of early-exit case

CRITICAL FINDING:
=================
The umem_mag_init_fast() SIMD implementation is SLOWER than scalar
for most magazine sizes, including the common sizes (15, 31, 47, 63).
This is likely due to:
  - Unaligned stores being slower than expected
  - Memory ordering or cache effects
  - Compiler optimization of memset being very good

RECOMMENDED ACTIONS:
====================
1. Add threshold to umem_mag_scan_notnull:
   #define UMEM_SIMD_SCAN_THRESHOLD 4  (or 8 for conservative)

2. Add threshold to umem_mag_init_fast:
   #define UMEM_SIMD_INIT_THRESHOLD 64  (or just use memset always)

3. Consider removing SIMD from init entirely - memset is faster\!

4. Verify results on other platforms (SSE2, NEON, AMD CPU)

This validates Sun Microsystems reviewer concern #5\!
